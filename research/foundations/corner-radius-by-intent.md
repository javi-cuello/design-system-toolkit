# Corner radius by intent

*How to build a radius model that says what a corner is for as well as how round it is: the scale, the roles on top of it, how components, layouts and themes use it, how it reaches Figma and three platforms, and how to get there from the radii a library already has.*

A product team lines up its form controls for a review. The button is 6px round, the text input 4px, the select 8px, and the date picker someone built last spring 5px. A card at 16px holds a cover image that is also 16px, and its corners look swollen. On phones, the bottom sheet keeps its rounded bottom corners pressed against the edge of the screen. Then the brand team asks for everything to be "a little softer" next quarter.

None of those values is wrong on its own. What is missing is a model: a short scale, a few named roles that say which corners belong together, and rules for how corners behave inside other corners, at the edge of a screen and in a softer theme.

# Part one: the model

## The radius scale

Keep one short scale of six to ten steps, from none to full, and name the steps so a new one can be added without renaming the rest.

The library in the opening has four radii for four controls because each was chosen by eye on the day it was built. A scale ends that: it is the list of values a corner may take, and nothing else. The real question is how long the list is and what each step is called.

Shipping scales are short. Primer has four steps plus a default (3, 6, 12px and full); Atlassian eight (2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16px, full and a 25% tile); Polaris ten; Material ten since early 2025; Fluent eleven, up to 40px. Compare that with spacing scales, which run to fifteen or more: corners need fewer distinctions, because a reader can tell 4px from 6px on a button but rarely 24px from 28px on a dialog.

Names come in three schemes, the same three as for spacing. Here they are on one scale.

Each column is one step, drawn as its top-left corner at twice size. Under it are the three ways systems name it: t-shirt words (Atlassian `radius.medium`, Primer `borderRadius.medium`, Material `corner.medium`), names that encode the value (Carbon v12 `border-radius-04`, Polaris `border-radius-100` for 4px), and plain indexes (Radix `--radius-3`, Spectrum `corner-radius-300`).

The scheme matters most when a step arrives later. Atlassian's 2025 scale added 6px between 4 and 8 for buttons and inputs, a step its old numeric scale had no slot for. Material added `large-increased` (20dp) and `extra-large-increased` (32dp) in January 2025, wedged between existing t-shirt names. An index scheme has it worst: Spectrum 2 reused `corner-radius-200` for 5px when it had meant 8px in Spectrum 1, so the same name now draws a different corner.

> **Aside.** Polaris went through all three schemes in three releases: `base` and `large` (semantic), then `-1` to `-6` (index), then `-050` to `-750` (value-encoded, 100 = 4px). Each move shipped with a codemod, and the last one turned `-6` into `-750` because an index could not say 30px.

Two systems keep no scale at all, and both are worth knowing. GOV.UK writes `border-radius: 0` on buttons and inputs and has no radius tokens; Carbon v11 did the same until v12 added seven. At the other end, shadcn/ui keeps one variable, `--radius`, and derives the rest from it.

**Keep six to ten steps from `none` to `full`, and name them by t-shirt words or by value, not by index, so a step added later slots in without renaming its neighbours.** *Weight: moderate.* Ten of the twelve scales read here have six to eleven steps (Primer, with four, and Ant Design, with five, are shorter), and the two index schemes with a history here, Polaris's and Spectrum's, were renamed or reused.

## Roles on top

Put a small role layer over the scale (detail, control, container, overlay, full) so that every button, input and select takes one token, and a change to controls is one edit.

Back to the review: the button, input, select and date picker are four controls with four radii. A primitive scale alone does not stop that, because `radius.small` and `radius.medium` are both valid choices for an input. What stops it is a token that names the job: `radius.control`, used by everything a person presses or types into.

Atlassian does this inside its token descriptions rather than as a second set of tokens. Each radius says what it is for: `radius.xsmall` for badges and checkboxes, `radius.small` for tags and tooltips, `radius.medium` for buttons, inputs and selects, `radius.large` for cards and menus, `radius.xlarge` for modals and tables, `radius.full` for avatars and other people-related elements. Five of the six components sit on a different step, and none of them chose its own: the role decided. Primer reaches the same place with a single alias, `borderRadius.default` pointing at `medium`, described as the preferred radius for standard components. Material maps every component's `container-shape` to a step of its scale (a filled card to `corner-medium`, a dialog to `corner-extra-large`, a text field to `corner-extra-small`).

Carbon v12 adds something the others do not: the context sets the role. Buttons read their radius from `--cds-button-radius`, which defaults to a pill, and a container such as a toolbar, a modal footer or a side panel sets it to `$border-radius-00`, so every button inside goes square at once.

The worked case uses illustrative role names. Here is the review's form, before and after.

```css
/* Before: each control picks a step */
.button { border-radius: var(--radius-6); }
.input  { border-radius: var(--radius-4); }
.select { border-radius: var(--radius-8); }

/* After: one role, one edit */
:root { --radius-control: var(--radius-6); }
.button, .input, .select, .datepicker { border-radius: var(--radius-control); }
```

The before needs four edits to make controls rounder, and a fifth control will pick a fifth value. The after needs one line.

**Add roles for the groups that must change together (detail, control, container, overlay and full cover most libraries), point each at a scale step, and have components use only roles.** *Weight: strong.* Atlassian, Primer, Material and Carbon all assign radius by job, in descriptions, an alias, component tokens or context, and none of them leaves the choice to each component.

## Full and pill

Make "full" a very large fixed value such as `9999px`, never `50%`, and never mix it with small radii on the same box.

A Subscribe button is 160px wide and 44px tall, and the design calls for round ends. The obvious CSS is `border-radius: 50%`. See what each value draws.

The left button is an oval. CSS resolves a percentage against each axis separately: 50% of the width is 80px and 50% of the height is 22px, so every corner is an 80 by 22 ellipse. On the right, `9999px` is far too big for the box, and that is the point. The CSS backgrounds specification says corner curves must not overlap: when the radii on a side add up to more than its length, every radius is scaled down by the same factor. Here that factor brings 9999 to 22, half the height, which is exactly a pill, at any width.

That is why Primer, Polaris, Material Web and Radix all ship full as `9999px`, Fluent as `10000px` and Carbon v12 as `999999px`. Atlassian's site lists its `radius.full` as 999px while its package ships 9999px; either works for anything shorter than about 1000px.

The same scaling rule has a sharp edge. A tab wants pill-round top corners and 8px bottom corners, and someone writes `9999px 9999px 8px 8px`.

When roundness is part of the brand, make it one multiplier over the scale rather than a second scale per theme.

## What users notice

Nothing in the published research says one radius is easier to use than another; choose radius for consistency and meaning, and test the places where shape carries meaning.

The brand team's request comes with a claim that rounder means friendlier. The nearest evidence is psychology, not usability. Bar and Neta's 2006 study found that people preferred curved versions of everyday objects and abstract patterns to sharp-angled ones, and later work (Bertamini and colleagues in 2016) found the preference was for curvature itself, not only against angles. All of it uses objects and shapes, not interfaces. No published study read here varies corner radius alone on controls and measures recognition, speed or errors; Nielsen Norman Group's advice to keep buttons as rounded rectangles is guidance, not a test.

GOV.UK's story shows how far that evidence goes. In October 2025 its team proposed rounded buttons to make the brand "friendlier", argued they would add a fourth visual cue to buttons, and the team's earlier recommendation called it a usability improvement. After community feedback, the change was taken out of version 6 in December 2025.

Shape does carry meaning in a few places, and those are worth testing: a checkbox must not become round (Radix never rounds it fully "to prevent any confusion between it and a Radio"; Nielsen Norman Group says never use circles as checkboxes), and avatars use circles for people and squares for teams and projects (see [avatars and images](#avatars-and-images)).

Accessibility has one geometric rule that radius touches. WCAG 2.2's target size criterion measures whether a 24 by 24 square fits inside the target, and its own understanding page notes that rounded corners can leave too little room.

# Part three: platforms and tools

## One model, three platforms

A radius token is one number with a unit; each platform's build turns it into px, dp or points, and the trap is a converter that assumes rem.

The same review card ships on web, Android and iOS. In Figma the button is bound to `radius.control`; the web team writes `border-radius: 6px`, Android `6.dp` and iOS `6`. Three numbers that agree today and three places to forget when the theme gets rounder.

The token format is the common ground. The W3C design tokens format (2025.10) has no radius type: a radius is a `dimension`, a value and a unit (px or rem), and no composite type carries one (a `border` holds colour, width and style only). Then the unit itself. Polaris, Primer and Atlassian ship radius in rem (Primer's `--borderRadius-medium` is 0.375rem, and even `full` is 624.9375rem); Spectrum and Material ship px and dp. The two tiles look similar at full size; the enlarged corner on the right shows the difference. The squircle (a superellipse with exponent 2) stays inside the same 40px box but bulges toward the corner and meets the edge more gently. CSS gets it through `corner-shape: squircle`, from the Borders and Box Decorations 4 draft, which ships in Chrome 139 and later; Safari has it only in a beta and Firefox only in Nightly, so in other browsers the corner falls back to `round`.

The tools do not agree on the curve. Figma's corner smoothing (60% matches iOS) is not a superellipse: it is built from Bézier segments that can extend along the edge up to twice the radius. iOS's `.continuous` style is Apple's own curve, now the default for SwiftUI rounded rectangles. CSS's squircle stays inside the radius box. The same number of pixels gives three slightly different corners, which is why the Figma-to-CSS gap is covered in [where Figma and CSS render differently](../figma-and-code/where-figma-and-css-render-differently.md#radii-and-corner-smoothing).

**Keep the radius value in the token and the corner style out of it: use `.continuous` on iOS, `corner-shape: squircle` on the web as an enhancement where Chrome shows it, and Figma smoothing only to preview iOS, never as a value a token carries.** *Weight: moderate.* Figma cannot bind smoothing to a variable, and the three curves differ by construction.

## Radius in Figma variables

Put radius in two collections, primitives hidden and roles scoped to corner radius, give each role code syntax, and bind every corner field to a role.

The review's designers set radii by typing numbers into the corner field. Figma variables make the role the only thing they can pick. The model above maps directly.

```
Primitives (one mode, hidden from publishing, no scopes)
  radius/0  radius/2  radius/4  radius/6  radius/8  radius/12  radius/16  radius/9999

Shape (modes: Default, Rounder; scope: Corner radius)
  radius/detail     → radius/2   | radius/4
  radius/control    → radius/6   | radius/8
  radius/container  → radius/8   | radius/12
  radius/overlay    → radius/12  | radius/16
  radius/full       → radius/9999| radius/9999
```

The corner radius field, and each independent corner, shows a variable chip. Corner smoothing has none: it is not one of the fields a variable can bind. The variable is scoped to Corner radius, so it appears in corner pickers and nowhere else, and its code syntax carries one name per platform.

To set this up in a file (Figma as of November 2026; menu labels move between releases):

1. Open the variables panel and create a collection named `Primitives` with one mode. Add a number variable per step: `radius/0`, `radius/2`, up to `radius/16`, and `radius/9999`.
2. Select all of them, edit them together, and clear every scope, so no picker offers a primitive.
3. Mark the collection hidden from publishing; aliases into it keep working.
4. Create a second collection, `Shape`. Rename its first mode `Default`, and add a `Rounder` mode if the brand needs one.
5. Add each role as a number variable (`radius/detail`, `radius/control`, `radius/container`, `radius/overlay`, `radius/full`) and, in each mode's cell, choose an alias to a primitive instead of typing a number.
6. Edit each role: under scoping, tick Corner radius only; under code syntax, enter the Web, Android and iOS names the build generates.
7. On each component, select the frame and, in the appearance section, bind the corner radius to a role through the variable picker; for a group or a sheet, open independent corners and bind each corner, leaving the square ones at `radius/0`.
8. To preview the Rounder theme, set the page's or a frame's mode for the `Shape` collection to Rounder; every bound corner follows.

Two limits are worth knowing. A Tokens Studio radius with several values (`8px 8px 0 0`) is skipped on export to variables, because a variable holds one number, so per-corner shapes are four bindings, not one token. And independent corners cannot be changed on an instance, so a component that needs a squared variant needs it as a variant.

**Set up two collections, primitives hidden and unscoped, roles scoped to Corner radius with code syntax, and bind every corner of every component to a role, with a mode for any rounder theme.** *Weight: moderate.* The scope, the per-corner binding and the missing smoothing binding are in Figma's plugin API; the two-collection shape is the same one that works for spacing.

## Cheat sheet

| You have | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Button 6, input 4, select 8 | One `radius.control` for all three |
| `border-radius: 50%` on a button | `radius.full` = `9999px` |
| `9999px 9999px 8px 8px` | Half the control height on top, 8 below |
| Card 16, padding 8, image 16 | Image `calc(16px - 8px)` = 8 |
| Focus ring at the button's radius | Radius plus offset, or `outline` |
| `border-top-left-radius` on a side sheet | `border-start-start-radius` |
| Rounded sheet against the screen edge | Radius 0 on the edge side |
| Square avatar for a person | Circle for people, square for the rest |
| `overflow: hidden` to clip a card image | `overflow: clip` |
| `size/remToDp` on a px radius | `size/dp` or a custom transform |
| Corner smoothing as a token | `.continuous` on iOS, `corner-shape` on the web |
| Typed radii in Figma | Bind to a role scoped to Corner radius |
| A second scale for a rounder brand | One factor, or one Rounder mode |

## The short version

Give radius a short scale from none to full, six to ten steps named by t-shirt words or by value, and put a few roles on top: detail, control, container, overlay and full. Components use roles only, so every control shares one radius and a change is one edit (strong). Write full as 9999px on the web, never 50%, and never mix it with small corners on one box (strong).

Inside a rounded container, the inner radius is the outer radius minus the padding, and a focus ring outside a component is its radius plus the offset (strong). Write per-corner radii with logical names so right-to-left mirrors them, and square any side that meets the screen edge rather than shrinking radius by breakpoint (moderate to strong). Circles mean people; rounded squares mean everything else (strong).

Store radius as a DTCG dimension in px and build it with px-aware transforms; whether CSS takes px or rem is contested, and this toolkit keeps px. Treat continuous corners as a platform finish, not a token value (moderate). In Figma, keep primitives hidden and roles scoped to Corner radius with code syntax, and put a rounder brand in a mode (moderate).

To get there, audit first, ship renames with a mapping before any value change, then turn the lint to error, and generate the docs from the tokens. Write down for engineering the role mapping, the nested and focus arithmetic, and which corners each sheet and group squares. Nothing published shows one radius is more usable than another, so choose for consistency and meaning (moderate).

## What to re-check later

Researched November 2026 against Atlassian `@atlaskit/tokens` 20.12.0, Primer Primitives 11.10.0, Polaris tokens 9.4.2 (its repository is archived), Material Web tokens v34 and Compose Material 3, Fluent tokens on `master`, Spectrum tokens 15.7.0, Ant Design `master`, TDesign, Radix Themes, shadcn/ui, SLDS 2, Carbon `main` (v12 in alpha) and GOV.UK Frontend.

- **Carbon v12** is in alpha; its radius token names may change (an open issue proposes `corner-radius`), and buttons default to pills only behind its v12 flag.
- **`corner-shape`** ships in Chrome 139 and later; Safari 27.3 is in beta and Firefox only in Nightly. Re-check before relying on it outside Chrome.
- **Docs against code**: Atlassian's `radius.full` (999 against 9999px) and Fluent's Large and X-Large (8 and 12 against 6 and 8px) were mismatched in November 2026.
- **Material's site** pages are rendered by script and could not be read; Material facts come from its token and Compose sources. The link between Material's January 2025 shape additions and M3 Expressive is inferred from dates.
- **Chrome's version** for outlines following `border-radius` (94) comes from a caniuse issue, not a Chromium release note.
- **Rem radii**: no written reason was found from Polaris, Primer or Atlassian.
- **SwiftUI's default corner style** is `.continuous` in today's documentation; when it changed from `.circular` was not found.
- **The squircle figure** draws CSS's superellipse; Figma's smoothing and iOS's continuous curve were not drawn or compared visually.
- **Figma**: the setup steps and the two panel figures are sketches drawn from Figma's plugin API and help pages, not screenshots, and the audit tally's counts are illustrative. The report of Check designs suggesting border-width variables comes from Figma's forum.
- **Unverified and left out**: Ant Design's reason for moving from 2px to 6px, the exact shadcn commit history before February 2026, Salesforce's reason for renaming its hooks, Slack's avatar rule, and the two touchscreen ergonomics papers whose abstracts could not be read.
