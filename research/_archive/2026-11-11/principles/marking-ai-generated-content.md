# Marking AI-generated content

How an interface should tell people which content a model produced, now that several design systems publish a pattern for it and the EU requires it.

Researched October 2026, when Carbon for AI was the only design system found with a published pattern. Re-checked and widened in November 2026: Carbon's Carbon for AI guidelines were re-read (its AI label component page has moved to `/components/ai-label/guidelines/`; the old `/usage/` URL returns 404), and four more systems were found and read: AWS Cloudscape's generative AI output label, SAP Fiori's local AI notice (version 1.145), Red Hat's AI transparency notices, and, through search only, the Lily design system's headless AI label. The legal position was re-checked against a Goodwin law-firm alert of August 2026 and the article on the AI Act reference site; the Official Journal text of the Digital Omnibus was not read directly. Searches for Salesforce, Atlassian, Fluent and Primer found no published rule for labelling AI output, which is not proof that none exists. The UK Intelligence Community Design System has an AI pattern page whose body did not render to a fetcher.

---

## What this answers

A tool that drafts component descriptions, writes audit findings or summarises a page with a model is showing people text nobody wrote. The question is not whether to say so; the systems that have thought about it agree, and so does EU law. The questions are how:

- What does the marker look like, and what does it say (section 1)?
- Where does it go, and how often (section 2)?
- Does the marker change when a person reviews or edits the text (section 3)?
- What does the law now require (section 4)?
- What should a toolkit build (section 5)?

The thesis: one short verbal label, placed at the lowest heading that contains all the AI output, rendered from a provenance field in data, carrying its review state, with any "AI look" reserved for that label alone.

---

## 1. A word, not just a sparkle

**The short answer.** Use a short text label. An icon may accompany it, never replace it.

The systems that publish a pattern all lead with words. Cloudscape's label reads "Generated by AI" ([Cloudscape, generative AI output label](https://cloudscape.design/patterns/genai/output-label/)). SAP Fiori's standard text is "Created with AI. Verify before use.", shortened to "Created with AI." only where space is limited, and it "must never be truncated" ([SAP Fiori, local AI notice](https://www.sap.com/design-system/fiori-design-web/v1-145/ui-elements/local-ai-notice/usage)). Red Hat is the most explicit: "At minimum use one visual and one verbal indicator," with wording such as "[Feature name] uses AI. Check for mistakes." ([Red Hat, AI transparency notices](https://ux.redhat.com/ai-guidelines/transparency-notices/)). Carbon's AI label is a component, "the primary indicator to communicate that AI is present, and it's how users can access explainability content" ([Carbon for AI](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/carbon-for-ai/)).

The sparkle has become the common glyph, and Cloudscape reserves it: the sparkle-icon label is not for "other forms of AI that aren't generative." That reservation is the point. A glyph only carries meaning while nothing else uses it.

The toolkit's content page states the accessibility consequence: the label is text, and an icon-only marker needs an accessible name (`knowledge/ui/design/content.md#ai-disclosure`).

| System | Label text | Icon | Opens an explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon | AI label component | Yes | Yes, an explainability popover, required on every AI component |
| Cloudscape | "Generated by AI" | Sparkle, generative AI only | Not specified |
| SAP Fiori | "Created with AI. Verify before use." | Not specified in the label | Interactive variant: popover, then a help page |
| Red Hat | "[Feature] uses AI. Check for mistakes." and others | Required alongside text | Persistent notice under an "i" icon in assistants |
| Lily | Headless inline indicator | Consumer's choice | Recommends wrapping in a button or popover |

## 2. One label, at the lowest heading that holds all of it

**The short answer.** Place the label next to the output, once per region, at the lowest container that holds every AI result. Do not label each item.

Cloudscape asks for "close proximity to the output" and warns against redundant labels: when every item in a list is AI-generated, label the group header, not each item. SAP sets the rule most precisely: one label per screen, "next to the lowest title element that contains all AI results." It also scales placement to impact. In high and very high impact decisions the label goes between the section header and the content, and the interactive variant is recommended; in low and medium impact cases it may sit at the end of the content area, with an invisible region label such as "<region name>. Contains AI-generated content." for assistive technology.

Over-labelling is the failure the systems keep naming. A label on every row becomes wallpaper, and wallpaper is ignored exactly where the reader most needs to notice it.

---

## 3. The label carries state

**The short answer.** AI provenance is a state, not a decoration. It changes when a person edits or reviews the text, so it must be rendered from data.

Carbon is the clearest. When a person overrides AI-suggested content, the component switches from the AI variant to the default one, and "the user should still be able to switch back to the initially AI-generated content via a revert to AI button" ([Carbon for AI](https://carbondesignsystem.com/guidelines/carbon-for-ai/)). SAP's read-only variant exists for the case where "no details on how AI results were validated can be provided", which implies the interactive one should say how they were.

The toolkit adds the review state, as its own rule for its own generated text: a model-drafted string reads "Drafted by AI, not yet reviewed" until a recorded review clears it to "Drafted by AI, reviewed by <name>", and template-generated text carries no label at all, because it is not AI-generated and over-labelling devalues the label (`knowledge/ui/design/content.md#ai-disclosure`). Each string's source (template, model, person) and review state is a field in data, and the label renders from it, never by hand. A label that never changes after review "has become wallpaper; wire it to the review record."

---

## 4. The AI look is for AI only

**The short answer.** If AI content gets its own visual style, use it nowhere else.

Carbon styles AI with "light-inspired effects like brightness, glow, and gradients," a metaphor "to 'illuminate' AI-generated content," and forbids borrowing it: "Don't use the Carbon for AI styling as decoration." Glow and gradient are exactly the decoration a design standard otherwise refuses. The trade is defensible only while the style means one thing, and it is checkable: the AI style tokens appear only inside AI-labelled components.

---

## 5. Since 2 August 2026, the EU requires it

**The short answer.** Article 50 of the EU AI Act applies from 2 August 2026 and was not postponed by the Digital Omnibus. Generative systems already on the market before that date have until 2 December 2026 to add machine-readable marking.

Article 50 requires that people be "informed that they are interacting with an AI system" unless it is obvious, and that providers of systems generating synthetic text, images, audio or video ensure outputs "are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated" ([AI Act, Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in force from 27 July 2026, moved high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I), but left transparency in place, with one transitional period: generative systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 for the marking duty. The Commission adopted guidelines on the transparency obligations on 20 July 2026 ([Goodwin, August 2026](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force)).

SAP Fiori now cites Article 50 directly and calls its notice mandatory for "all user interfaces that involve AI-generated or AI-edited information."

Two cautions. The law's machine-readable marking is a property of the output file or stream, not the visible label this article is about; a visible label does not satisfy it and it does not replace a visible label. And whether a design toolkit's generated audit text falls within Article 50 is a legal question this article does not answer. The design answer is the same either way.

## 6. Cheat sheet

| Decision | Do | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| Marker | Short text label, icon optional | Icon alone, or a glow alone |
| Wording | "Generated by AI", "Drafted by AI, not yet reviewed" | Vague "Smart" or "Magic" names |
| Placement | Next to the output, once, at the lowest heading containing all of it | A label on every row |
| Impact | Above the content, interactive, for high-impact decisions | The same quiet footer label for a decision that matters |
| State | Rendered from a provenance field; changes on edit and review | A hand-written label that never changes |
| Template output | No label | Labelling deterministic text as AI |
| Style | AI style only inside labelled regions | Borrowing the AI glow as decoration |
| Explanation | Label opens an explanation on request | An explanation forced on every view |

---

## 7. The short version

Say it in words: "Generated by AI", with a sparkle beside it if you like, never a sparkle alone. Put one label next to the output, at the lowest heading that holds all of it, and raise it above the content when the decision matters. Render it from data, so that it changes when a person edits or reviews the text, and leave template text unlabelled. Keep any AI visual style for the label alone. In the EU this is no longer optional: Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026.
