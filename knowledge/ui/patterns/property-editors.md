# Property editors: inspectors and property definitions

How a tool lets people define and edit the properties of a thing: a component's props, a field's settings, a column's type. Two jobs share the pattern and are kept apart here. *Setting values* is editing the properties of the selected item (an instance's label, a button's size). *Defining the schema* is creating or changing the properties themselves (adding a "Size" property, its options and its default). Most tools put the first in a side inspector and the second in a dialog or a menu opened from it. It serves the principles *people in control, mistakes forgiven* (a change says what it will do to existing items before it does it) and *efficient for people who know it* (the inspector follows the selection, the work stays in view). No design-standard rule is admitted for property editors yet. Fields and their errors are [forms](forms.md)'s; when a change saves is [settings: saving settings](settings.md#saving-settings) and [interaction: saving, explicit or automatic](../design/interaction.md#saving-explicit-or-automatic); the surfaces are [dialogs](dialogs.md) and [sidebars: contextual side panels](sidebars.md#contextual-side-panels), and showing or hiding the inspector is [sidebars: showing and hiding panels in a tool layout](sidebars.md#showing-and-hiding-panels-in-a-tool-layout); the controls are [selection](selection.md).

## Property editors

**Use when / don't use**
- Use a **side inspector** for setting values on whatever is selected, when people move between items and want the canvas or list in view. It shows one item's properties and follows the selection. Figma's Properties section in the right panel, Framer's properties panel, Plasmic's right sidebar and Retool's Inspector ("the panel you most interact with when building") all work this way; Apple: an inspector "displays the details of the currently selected item, automatically updating its contents when the item changes".
- Use a **dialog** for defining or redefining a property: a short, infrequent, self-contained task with a clear commit. Figma creates a property through a "Create component property" modal (name, default value, then "Create property"); Airtable edits a field's name, type and default and ends with Save. Carbon: modals suit "short and non-frequent tasks, such as editing or management tasks", and "if a user needs to repeatably perform a task, consider making the task completable on the main page".
- Use an **inline menu** in place for a quick change to one property of a list or table people are already looking at. Notion edits a property by clicking it in the database ("a menu where you can edit the property's name, filter or sort by that property"); Airtable does the same from a column's dropdown. Atlassian: inline edit is "for an editable field that is not part of a form".
- A **list of rows** is the overview in all three: each property one row (name, type, current or default value), reorderable, each row opening its editor. Figma's Properties section, Notion's "Edit properties" list and Storybook's controls table are such lists.
- Don't put a long schema in a modal with steps; Atlassian: "avoid multiple steps that require navigation within the modal dialog". Past a handful of fields per property, or when properties depend on each other, give the schema its own page or pane.
- Don't use a floating panel for typing-heavy work. Apple: "prefer simple adjustment controls in a panel… avoid including controls that require typing text".

**How**
- **Rows first, detail on demand.** The list says each property's name, type and value or default at a glance; editing one opens its detail in the chosen surface. On hover over a property row Figma highlights the matching layer on the canvas "so you know what you're editing"; give the same link on focus, not hover alone.
- **Group by what the person is setting.** Retool organises the inspector into sections such as Content, Interaction and Appearance, with "less frequently used settings… grouped into subsections to reduce clutter"; Webflow code components take a `group` per prop ("Consider how props will appear together in the designer"); Storybook has `table.category` and `table.subcategory`; Framer's Object control nests related props under one titled button. Keep one fixed order of kinds where the tool has one: in Figma "variant properties always sit above other property types".
- **Progressive disclosure, two kinds.** *Advanced*: rare props collapsed by default (Plasmic `advanced`, "to reduce UI clutter"; Builder.io `advanced`, under a "Show More" section). *Conditional*: a control appears only when another value makes it relevant (Framer `hidden(props)`, Storybook `if: { arg: 'advanced' }`, Plasmic `hidden`, Builder.io `showIf`). Hide a dependent control; don't disable it without saying why.
- **Each row carries its help.** A one-line description under the name (Plasmic `helpText`, "always shown right below the name"; Builder.io `helperText`; Framer `description`). Names short and in the person's words (Webflow: "keep them short and title case. Use descriptions to provide more context").
- **Type is chosen first, and the type decides the editor.** Figma picks the type (Boolean, Instance swap, Text, Variant, Slot) before the dialog opens; Airtable picks the type, then the name. Each type brings its own fields (an Airtable Number field adds precision, negatives and a default). Builder.io: "Types correlate to what editing UI is appropriate to edit this field."
- **Changing type keeps the item and converts its values.** Airtable converts existing values, keeps everything that points at the field ("converting an existing field is almost always better than building a replacement"), offers "Show dependencies" first, and says when a conversion clears values. Say before the change what will be lost, and how many values; confirm only when something is ([interaction: undo or confirmation](../design/interaction.md#undo-or-confirmation)). Where a change is impossible, say so in place: Figma switches a layer only to "an existing property of the same type".
- **Defaults are explicit and say where they apply.** Every property has a default in its definition (Webflow: "Always set meaningful default values so components work immediately when added to a page"). Say whether changing it reaches existing items: in Figma a new default reaches instances "if the associated instance layer has no property overrides"; in Airtable defaults "appear only when new records are created". Show an unset value's effective default as a placeholder (Plasmic `defaultValueHint`), never as an entered value.
- **Validate against the schema.** Constraints live in the definition and the editor enforces them: Framer `min`, `max`, `step`, `unit`, `maxLength`; Plasmic `required` shows "an error indicator" and `validator` returns "an error message to be displayed"; Builder.io `regex`. In an inspector a value is checked when it is committed (Enter or leaving the field); an invalid one keeps the typed text with its message beside it, and the item stays on its last good value ([interaction: error prevention and validation timing](../design/interaction.md#error-prevention-and-validation-timing)).
- **Instant apply in the inspector, an explicit commit in the dialog.** Inspector edits apply on commit with no Save: Figma renames on "return / enter or click outside the field". A definition dialog ends in one named button ("Create property", "Save") and Cancel. Never both models in one surface ([settings: saving settings](settings.md#saving-settings)).
- **Enum options are a list of their own.** Each option has a value and a display label (Framer `options` with `optionTitles`; Storybook `labels`; Plasmic and Builder.io `{ value, label }`). Options are added by typing and Enter (Notion), edited in place, and reordered by a drag handle (Figma: "Hover over a value to reveal handles. Click and drag to reorder") with a non-drag alternative. The listed order is the order shown wherever the property is used, so the author sets it on purpose. Offer the enum's control by count: a segmented control or inline radios for a few (Framer `displaySegmentedControl`, Storybook `inline-radio`), a select for more ([selection: choosing the control](selection.md#choosing-the-control)).
- **A long list of choices gets a searchable picker, curated first.** Figma's instance swap shows "preferred instances" by default, with search to reach the rest; its variable picker searches "by variable name or variable group" and filters by library. Narrow the list to what fits the property before search: a Figma variable's scope means it "won't appear as an option for any other supported properties". Colours show as swatches beside their names (Figma draws variables as squares, styles as circles); Plasmic can return the token reference (`var(--TOKEN_NAME)`) rather than a raw hex, which keeps the binding.
- **One row height, one label style, one header style.** Every row in the inspector is the tool's control height (32px, 24px in compact; [visual: quiet chrome in a dense tool](../design/visual.md#quiet-chrome-in-a-dense-tool)); a label in the secondary text colour at the small size (12px), the value in the primary colour at the body size. A section's header is a row of the same height: its title in sentence case at the body size and a heavier weight, its actions (add, more) as 24px icon buttons at the trailing edge, and the whole header the disclosure toggle when the section collapses. Sections part by one hairline or one larger gap, never by a card per section ([Apple boxes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/boxes): "adding nested boxes… can make your interface feel busy"). Panel side padding is one value for every section, so every label starts on one edge.
- **Labels may be short or iconic, never absent from the name.** Figma UI3 lets people turn property labels off "for a more icon-forward view", with "visible labels match aria-labels used for screen readers" ([Figma, behind the UI3 redesign](https://www.figma.com/blog/behind-our-redesign-ui3/); [our approach to UI3](https://www.figma.com/blog/our-approach-to-designing-ui3/)). It also put "component controls like variants and instances… above attributes like color and size", merged everything about layout into one section, and went "back to the checkbox as the most clear, efficient control" for a boolean (the same posts).
- **A switch between modes inside the inspector is a segmented control,** never a second tab set inside a tab: Apple: "Consider using a segmented control to help people switch views in a toolbar or inspector pane" ([Apple segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls); [navigation: tabs](navigation.md#tabs)).
- **Tokens before raw values.** Where a property can take a token, the picker offers tokens first and a raw value second, and the row shows the token's name once bound, so a theme change reaches it ([code: structure, not screenshots](../../code/figma-handoff.md#structure-not-screenshots)).

**Cases**
- Adding a "Size" property opens a three-step wizard → one dialog: type chosen before it opens, then name, options and default, then "Create property".
- An inspector has 40 props in one flat list → sections by what they set, rare ones under Advanced, dependent ones shown only when their parent value makes them relevant.
- A select-type field is changed to text and its options vanish without warning → say what converts, what is lost and how many records it touches before applying; keep the field so its dependants keep working.
- Changing a default silently restyles every existing instance → say in the dialog whether the default reaches existing items, and which ones (those without overrides).
- Enum options can be reordered only by drag → add Move up and Move down (or Alt with the arrow keys) that keep focus on the moved option.
- A colour property opens 300 unnamed swatches → names beside swatches, search by name and group, scoped to colours that fit the property, the curated set first.
- Each inspector section sits in a bordered card with its own 16px padding inside a padded panel, and headings are 11px uppercase → drop the cards, one hairline between sections, headings sentence case at the body size in 600.
- Rows run 28, 32 and 36px in different sections → one row height for the inspector.
- An inspector field with an invalid number reverts with no message → keep the typed value, say the allowed range beside the field, leave the item on its last good value.

**Accessibility**
- 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (each section a heading, or a `fieldset` with a `legend`; each control labelled by the property name), 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (a custom swatch or segmented control exposes its name and state), 1.4.1 Use of Color (a swatch carries its name in text).
- 3.2.2 On Input: changing a value in the inspector does not move focus or change the selection; showing a conditional control is fine, moving focus to it is not ([Understanding 3.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/on-input.html)).
- 2.5.7 Dragging Movements: reordering options or properties by drag has a single-pointer alternative ([Understanding 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)). The APG's [rearrangeable listbox](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/listbox/examples/listbox-rearrangeable/) moves an option with Alt plus Up or Down Arrow and keeps focus on it, so "screen reader users… hear the position of an option after it is moved".
- The canvas highlight shown for a hovered row is also shown for a focused row (2.1.1 Keyboard).
- A dialog that defines a property follows [dialogs: focus](dialogs.md#focus-in-trapped-returned); focus returns to the new property's row on Create.

**Build**
- *Elements:* the inspector an `aside` with a heading naming the selection; each group a `section` with a heading, or a `details` for an Advanced group; each property a native control with a `label`; the property list a `ul` of rows; a definition editor a `dialog` holding a `form` with one submit button.
- *ARIA and keyboard:* native controls carry it; an options list that reorders follows APG listbox with Move up, Move down, Add and Remove; a searchable picker follows [APG combobox](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/combobox/) with a listbox popup ([code: selection controls past the native set](../../code/accessibility.md#selection-controls-past-the-native-set)); a row's help is linked with `aria-describedby`. Dragging properties and the non-drag route are [code: drop targets, positions and trees](../../code/javascript.md#drop-targets-positions-and-trees).
- *States:* unset (the placeholder shows the default), set, overridden (differs from the default, with a reset), bound to a token (the token's name shown), invalid (the message beside the field), read-only (and why).
- *Data:* render the editor from the schema, not by hand: each property's type, label, help, group, default, constraints, options and visibility condition are fields of one definition, as every tool sourced here does (Framer `addPropertyControls`, Storybook `argTypes`, Plasmic `registerComponent`, Builder.io inputs, Webflow `declareComponent`).
- *Rules:* code-standard rule 15 (help and errors are descriptions, not part of the name), code-standard rule 8 (each control is the native element inside any wrapper), code-standard rule 36 (one renderer reads the schema, not a hand-built form per type); a commit button is not disabled as a validity signal ([actions: disabled buttons](actions.md#disabled-buttons)).

**Check**
- *Script:* could check every inspector control has a label, every group a heading, every enum option a non-empty label and a unique value, every reorderable list a non-drag alternative, and every swatch an accessible name.
- *Judgement:* Is defining a property separated from setting its value? Does each surface (inspector, dialog, inline menu) have one save model? Does the person see, before a type or default change, what it will do to existing items?

## Read-only inspect list

**Use when / don't use**
- Use when the panel reports an item's values for reading and copying, not for changing: a component's resolved properties, a layer's styles, a contract's settled values. The model is Figma Dev Mode, whose inspect panel "displays a list of properties that are set for the layer" ([Figma, Guide to Dev Mode](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/15023124644247-Guide-to-Dev-Mode)); Chrome DevTools' Computed tab shows "only the CSS that's actually being applied to an element" ([Chrome DevTools CSS reference](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/css/reference)).
- Don't draw it as disabled inputs. Carbon: "Don't use a disabled state for components if they need to be read by the user", since "disabled states are not read by screen readers and do not pass visual contrast" ([Carbon read-only states](https://carbondesignsystem.com/patterns/read-only-states-pattern/)); Cloudscape: "Don't use key-value pairs to display read-only controls in forms" ([Cloudscape key-value pairs](https://cloudscape.design/components/key-value-pairs/)). A field the person could edit with permission is a `readonly` control; a value nobody edits here is text ([interaction: disabled, hidden or read-only](../design/interaction.md#disabled-hidden-or-read-only)).
- Where the same panel edits in one mode and inspects in another, the two are separate renderings of one schema, never the editor with every control greyed.

**How**
- **Two columns, label then value,** one property per row, in the editor's order and groups so a person moving between modes finds each value where they left it. Labels in the secondary text colour, values in the primary at full contrast (Carbon: read-only text "should still pass 4.5:1"); the label column a fixed width from the longest label, about a third of the panel; Cloudscape's minimum column is 150px and no source sets the label width.
- **Same density as the editor:** a row the height of the editor's control row, so switching modes moves nothing ([interaction: layout stability](../design/interaction.md#layout-stability)); groups keep their headings.
- **Whole values, never ellipses.** Cloudscape: "Don't collapse or truncate data using ellipses (...)… always show the full string for both label and value." Long values wrap within the value column.
- **A token shows its name first,** as a chip with its swatch or a mono name, the resolved value beside it or on demand; in Dev Mode a person can "click on the variable name in the Inspect panel to open the Variable details modal" ([Figma, variables in Dev Mode](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/27882809912471)). A raw value with no token reads differently from a bound one, so an unbound value is visible as such.
- **Copy on the value.** Figma: "You can click on the values to copy them to your clipboard"; Cloudscape offers an inline copy "to quickly copy a string of text" ([Cloudscape copy to clipboard](https://cloudscape.design/components/copy-to-clipboard/)). The copy target is a real button, named "Copy <label>", with a short confirmation ("Copied") announced politely; copying never selects or changes the item.
- **Unset is shown, not hidden,** when the reader needs to know a property is at its default: "Default" or the default value in the secondary colour; a property that cannot apply to this item is left out (design-standard rule 5). DevTools hides unset ones until "Show All" is checked.
- **A code view is a second tab, not a second list:** Dev Mode toggles to Code, where "the inspect panel displays autogenerated code snippets for the layer" (Guide to Dev Mode).

**Cases**
- A view-only contract renders the editor's selects and switches greyed out → a label-value list in the same order and row height, values as text, tokens as named chips.
- A long token path is cut to "color.backgr…" → wrap it in the value column; the full name is what gets copied.
- A copy icon appears on hover only → a button always present on the row, or the value itself a button; reachable by Tab.
- An inspect panel lists 60 properties with half at default → group as the editor does; defaults shown quietly, or collapsed under a "Show all" as DevTools does.

**Accessibility**
- 1.3.1 Info and Relationships (each label tied to its value by markup), 1.4.3 Contrast (values are content, not inactive controls), 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value (each copy button named for its value), 4.1.3 Status Messages ("Copied" announced without moving focus), 2.1.1 Keyboard (copy reachable without a pointer).

**Build**
- *Elements:* a `dl`, each row a `div` holding one `dt` and one `dd` (HTML allows the wrapper, which carries the row's grid); one `dl` per group under a heading. GOV.UK's summary list "uses the description list (`<dl>`) HTML element" ([GOV.UK summary list](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/summary-list/)). A token value is text inside the `dd`, its swatch decorative (`aria-hidden`) beside the name.
- *Layout:* the `dl` a grid with `grid-template-columns: var(--inspect-label-width) 1fr` and `subgrid` rows, so labels align across groups; `overflow-wrap: anywhere` on values.
- *Copy:* a `button` in the `dd` calling `navigator.clipboard.writeText`, with a visually hidden live region for "Copied"; without script the value is still selectable text ([code: live regions and status messages](../../code/accessibility.md#live-regions-and-status-messages)).
- *Rules:* code-standard rule 9 (inspect and edit are two states of one renderer reading one schema), code-standard rule 15 (a value's note is a description), code-standard rule 18 (icon-only copy buttons named).

**Check**
- *Script:* could assert the panel in read-only mode has no `input`, `select` or `[disabled]`, every `dt` has a `dd`, no value is truncated with `text-overflow: ellipsis`, every copy button has a name, and each row's top matches the editor's row top within `interface.shiftTolerance`.
- *Judgement:* Is this person reading values to use elsewhere (inspect list) or changing them (editor)? Does each token read by its name?

**Sources:** Figma Help Center, Chrome DevTools, Carbon, Cloudscape and GOV.UK as linked. They agree on label-value rows, read-only values as full-contrast text, and copy on the value. No source sets the label column's width or the row height; the toolkit takes the editor's, so modes do not shift.

## Selecting on a canvas

**Use when / don't use**
- Use when a tool draws what is edited (a component, a frame, a page) and a part is chosen by pointing at it there; the inspector then follows that part.
- Don't use for a list or a tree: there a row is chosen directly, one level each.

**How**
- **Parent first, one level at a time.** A click chooses the outermost part under the pointer at the levels already open (the part chosen and every part it sits in); a second click, a double-click or Enter goes one level in; Shift+Enter goes back out. Figma: "when you click on an object that is part of a group or frame, we'll select the parent by default. Double-click on the object, or press the enter key, to select one level of nesting down". Sketch treats a group "as a single layer" until it is double-clicked.
- **A deep pick on a modifier.** ⌘-click (Ctrl on Windows) chooses the innermost part at once (Figma's deep select; Sketch's ⌘ while hovering).
- **Hover shows what a click would choose**, outlined thinner than the selection, so the next click is never a surprise; the outline follows the same parent-first rule, and the modifier too.
- **The outermost frame is labelled** with its name above it; pointing at or clicking the label is pointing at or clicking the frame (Figma's frame names).
- **Direct selection is the other model**: a click chooses the innermost part, Escape climbs out. Framer offers it as a preference (a third-party lesson summary says it is Framer's default; unconfirmed on Framer's own pages). Pick one model per tool; hover and click always share it.

**Cases**
- A button whose content row fills its outer box: hovering the label outlines the content row first, as a click would choose it, not the label.
- A tool with direct selection that outlines the outermost part on hover → the outline lies about the click; make them one rule.

**Accessibility**
- A canvas pick is a pointer path only; the same parts are reachable in the tree (or layers list), each a focusable row, and choosing a row chooses the part (2.1.1 Keyboard). Hovering a row outlines its part on the canvas, as Figma highlights a property's layer.

**Build**
- Find the part under the pointer, then walk up its containers until the next one up is open (chosen, or an ancestor of the chosen); that is the target. With ⌘ or Ctrl, the part under the pointer itself. One function serves hover, click and drag.
- Draw the hover and selection outlines on a layer over the canvas, not as the part's own outline: a part inside a rounded, clipping box would lose its own.

**Check**
- *Script:* could point at a nested part with nothing chosen and assert the outline and the click both land on its outermost container; click again and assert one level in; ⌘-click and assert the innermost.
- *Judgement:* does the outline always predict the click?

**Sources:** [Figma, Select layers and objects](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040449873) (parent by default, double-click or Enter one level down, deep select); [Sketch, Grouping layers](https://sketch.com/docs/designing/layer-basics/grouping-layers/) (a group as one layer, double-click in, ⌘ while hovering); Framer from a [lesson summary](https://youtubesummary.com/summary/AAhQd56msrQ) only, its own docs not found. No source documents the hover outline's width or colour; the toolkit draws it thinner than the selection's, in its colour.
